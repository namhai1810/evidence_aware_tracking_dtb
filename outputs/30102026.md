# Weekly Progress Report — HIVE HSV Ablation, ECIA Identity Recovery, and Panoramic Benchmark Pipeline

> Reporting window: work completed through **29 October 2026**. This document
> preserves the controlled HIVE + HSV ablation, records the subsequent HIVE +
> Lightweight ECIA identity-recovery experiment, and documents the dual-fisheye
> capture, offline stitching, person-benchmark bootstrap, and human-reviewed
> MOT dataset release.

## Executive status

- The HIVE + HSV experiment completed, but the added HSV bridge features did
  not generalize to the video-disjoint test. HIVE v2 remains the active
  historical-frame ranking baseline.
- Frozen HIVE v2 + ReID was extended with a 43,684-parameter Lightweight ECIA
  identity-recovery head. On four held-out QuadTrack videos, ECIA Full reduced
  ID switches from **92 to 45** (**−51.1%**) while improving IDF1 from
  **0.4476 to 0.4719** and HOTA from **0.3618 to 0.3783**. This result uses an
  oracle switch trigger and has not yet been reproduced on the new panorama
  benchmark.
- A frame-auditable dual-fisheye recording and offline stitching workflow is
  now operational. Five controlled scenarios produced **6,969 synchronized
  panorama frames** while preserving every decoded raw-camera frame.
- A first panorama person benchmark was bootstrapped with YOLO12l + ByteTrack:
  **11,918 candidate person boxes**, **154 predicted track IDs**, and **1,354
  evidence-backed review events**.
- Manual CVAT annotation is now complete for the trimmed five-sequence subset:
  **4,969 frames**, **8,518 verified person boxes**, and **10 sequence-local
  tracks**. Automated consistency checks found no invalid boxes, duplicate
  `(frame, track_id)` pairs, or disagreement with the manual detection export.
- The original 6,969-frame bootstrap remains pseudo-label data. Only the
  separately prepared 4,969-frame manual subset is treated as MOT ground truth.

> Decision: remove HSV bridge similarity from the active HIVE model and retain HIVE v2 as the current ranking baseline. Keep ECIA Full as the primary downstream identity-recovery research variant, while treating its current QuadTrack result as oracle-triggered rather than an end-to-end panorama result.

## 1. Experiment question

The ablation tested whether three LR-only color-similarity features improve historical-frame ranking:

- HSV similarity between each candidate and the aggregated old-track history;
- HSV similarity between each candidate and the LR target crop;
- HSV bridge similarity, defined as the minimum of the two similarities.

The features were computed entirely from LR crops. No HR image, HR detection, HR ReID embedding, GT identity, or label entered the model at inference.

HIVE + HSV used the same dataset, video split, seed, model variant, losses, optimizer, scheduler, batch size, and early-stopping configuration as HIVE v2. The controlled changes were the three additional metadata inputs and the resulting 48 additional trainable parameters (`146,251 → 146,299`).

## 2. Training and validation result

Training completed normally and stopped at epoch 11 after ten epochs without improving validation Success@1. The HSV checkpoint selected by validation was epoch 1; the HIVE v2 checkpoint was epoch 3.

| Validation metric | HIVE v2 | HIVE + HSV | HSV − v2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Success@1 | 64.47% (49/76) | **65.79% (50/76)** | +1.32 pp / +1 event |
| Success@3 | 76.32% | **78.95%** | +2.63 pp |
| Success@5 | 78.95% | **80.26%** | +1.31 pp |
| MRR | 71.28% | **73.05%** | +1.76 pp |
| NDCG@5 | 62.61% | **63.26%** | +0.65 pp |
| Oracle coverage | 89.47% | 89.47% | 0.00 pp |

The validation improvement was small: Success@1 increased by only one event. After epoch 1, training loss continued to decrease while validation ranking quality fell, showing rapid overfitting.

## 4. Candidate classification and calibration

| Test metric | HIVE v2 | HIVE + HSV | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Candidate accuracy | **71.47%** | 49.18% | −22.28 pp |
| Candidate precision | **50.65%** | 36.37% | −14.28 pp |
| Candidate recall | 86.59% | **98.88%** | +12.29 pp |
| Candidate F1 | **63.92%** | 53.18% | −10.74 pp |
| Event request rate | 94.57% | 100.00% | +5.43 pp |

HSV predicts nearly every candidate as positive: candidate recall becomes very high, but precision, accuracy, and F1 deteriorate. All 92 events cross the request threshold, indicating poor score calibration.

## 5. How HSV changed the selected frames

HSV changed the top-ranked candidate in 63/92 events:

- 9 events changed from incorrect under v2 to correct under HSV;
- 13 events changed from correct under v2 to incorrect under HSV;
- net result: four fewer correct top-one selections.

The selected history age shifted sharply toward the switch frame:

| Selection behavior | HIVE v2 | HIVE + HSV |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean seconds before switch | 1.438 s | 0.509 s |
| Median seconds before switch | 0.667 s | 0.300 s |
| Top-1 selections within 1 second | 59/92 | 85/92 |
| Top-1 selections at least 3 seconds back | 20/92 | 3/92 |

This is an observed correlation, not a standalone causal proof. However, under the controlled configuration, adding HSV changes optimization and produces a strong preference for very recent candidates while reducing held-out identity-ranking quality.

## 6. Inference cost

| Timing | HIVE v2 | HIVE + HSV | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Model forward | 0.815 ms/event | 0.783 ms/event | −0.033 ms |
| HSV feature extraction | — | 1.156 ms/event | +1.156 ms |
| Total selection inference | **0.815 ms/event** | **1.939 ms/event** | +1.124 ms |

The neural forward pass is similar, but histogram computation makes the HSV selection path approximately `2.38×` slower than HIVE v2.

## 7. Decision

HSV bridge similarity is rejected from the active HIVE model because it:

1. produces only a one-event validation gain;
2. loses four correct top-one events on the video-disjoint test;
3. lowers MRR and NDCG@5;
4. worsens candidate calibration;
5. introduces a strong recent-frame selection bias;
6. increases total selection latency by approximately 138%.

HIVE v2 remains the current baseline. The active dataset loader, model, training path, evaluator, configuration, and documentation no longer expose HSV model features.

This decision concerns HSV as a **direct HIVE input feature**. The v2 dataset builder still uses its existing LR-only appearance descriptor during offline candidate-pool proposal; that proposal stage is separate from the rejected neural input feature.

## HIVE + HSV conclusion

The HSV ablation is a useful negative result: it slightly improved validation but failed to generalize and increased inference cost. The evidence supports keeping the simpler HIVE v2 model and prioritizing features with stronger identity discrimination, better calibration, and less temporal shortcut risk.

## 9. HIVE v2 + Lightweight ECIA identity recovery on QuadTrack

### Protocol

HIVE v2 and `yolo26n-reid.onnx` were frozen and reused to construct identity
evidence for a new Lightweight ECIA association head. Only ECIA was optimized;
the model has **43,684 trainable parameters** and consumes frozen 512-D ReID
embeddings for Old, Target, one HIVE-selected HR crop, and at most 12 LR
competitor prototypes.

The experiment remains entirely on QuadTrack and uses a video-disjoint split:

| Role | QuadTrack sequences |
|---|---|
| Train | 0015, 0018, 0020, 0021, 0022, 0023, 0024, 0026, 0027, 0029 |
| Model validation / early stopping | 0019, 0028 |
| Operational threshold calibration | 0030 |
| Held-out test | 0016, 0017, 0025, 0031 |

All three ECIA checkpoints and probability/margin thresholds were frozen
before opening the held-out test. GT identity was used only for offline label
construction, calibration, and post-hoc evaluation; it was not a model input.
Every HIVE-based method used the same **92 held-out HR requests**, with at most
one HR crop per event.

The current protocol uses `oracle_switch_trigger=true`: it evaluates whether a
known switch event can be repaired, not whether the complete system can detect
an unknown ID switch online.

### Held-out tracking result

| Method | IDSW ↓ | IDF1 ↑ | HOTA ↑ | MOTA ↑ | Events immediately fixed | Segment-level wrong corrections |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LR-only | 92 | 0.4476 | 0.3618 | 0.3538 | 0 | 0 |
| HIVE v2 + HR ReID threshold baseline | 88 | 0.4503 | 0.3630 | 0.3541 | 4 | 0 |
| ECIA Base-only | 46 | 0.4706 | 0.3768 | 0.3567 | 48 | 4 |
| ECIA Base + HR Gain | **42** | 0.4432 | 0.3665 | **0.3570** | **53** | 5 |
| **ECIA Full (Gain + Gate)** | 45 | **0.4719** | **0.3783** | 0.3568 | 48 | **3** |

Relative to LR-only, ECIA Full reduces IDSW by **47 events / 51.1%**, increases
IDF1 by **0.0242 absolute**, and increases HOTA by **0.0165 absolute**. It
requested 53 merges; replay applied 48 and rejected five through the existing
collision/missing-segment safety check. All four held-out videos improved in
both IDSW and IDF1:

| Sequence | IDSW, LR → Full | IDF1, LR → Full |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0016 | 13 → 4 | 0.3016 → 0.3392 |
| 0017 | 17 → 11 | 0.3379 → 0.3707 |
| 0025 | 61 → 30 | 0.4099 → 0.4329 |
| 0031 | 1 → 0 | 0.9528 → 0.9573 |

“Immediately fixed” and “wrong correction” are not mutually exclusive: the
former checks the target switch point, whereas the latter detects harmful GT
identity contamination elsewhere in the affected replay segment. Final IDSW,
IDF1, and HOTA therefore remain the authoritative end-to-end replay metrics.

### Ablation interpretation and current limitations

The main improvement comes from learned LR identity association: Base-only
already reduces IDSW from 92 to 46. Gated HR evidence provides a smaller but
consistent increment over Base-only: one fewer IDSW, IDF1 `+0.0013`, HOTA
`+0.0015`, and one fewer wrong correction. In contrast, forcing HR Gain to full
strength gives the lowest IDSW (42) but lowers IDF1 below LR-only (`0.4432`),
showing that IDSW alone can reward harmful over-merging.

The Full gate has not yet learned a strongly separated HR-quality signal. HIVE
selected the correct historical identity in 58/92 test events; mean gate values
were 0.597 for correct HR selections and 0.588 for incorrect selections. This
small separation indicates that the current improvement also depends on the
base scores, learned Abstain logit, threshold calibration, and replay safety.

Training shows rapid overfitting. Best validation checkpoints occur at epoch 4
for Base-only and epoch 3 for both HR variants. By the final early-stopped
epochs, training loss falls near 0.02 while validation loss rises to 2.9–3.2.
Early stopping prevents using these later checkpoints, but the gap motivates
more diverse videos, stronger regularization, and repeated-seed evaluation.

The complete machine-readable result and report are preserved under
[`hive_ecia_experiment/comparison/`](./hive_ecia_experiment/comparison/).

## 10. Panoramic data-creation pipeline

### 10.1 Objective and workflow

The next stage moves from the public QuadTrack data to a controlled benchmark
captured with two dual-fisheye camera streams. The purpose is to create
panoramic sequences containing known tracking stress cases and retain enough
evidence to inspect false detections, missed detections, fragmented tracks, and
identity switches manually.

The implemented workflow is:

1. record both compressed camera streams in one FFmpeg process on a shared
   timeline;
2. preserve the two original tracks and split them without re-encoding;
3. validate frame PTS, monotonicity, continuity, camera-frame counts, and
   cross-camera skew;
4. write `frame_manifest.csv` with matched and unmatched camera frames;
5. export every decoded raw frame from both cameras;
6. stitch only explicitly synchronized frame pairs offline;
7. number stitched frames contiguously for MOT compatibility while retaining a
   mapping to both source-camera frame numbers;
8. run person detection and tracking on the stitched panorama images;
9. generate MOT/QuadTrack labels, YOLO labels, per-frame annotations, track
   summaries, and a human-review queue.

This separation is intentional: the low-latency live stream is not treated as
the authoritative benchmark recording, and an unmatched frame is never paired
with an arbitrary frame from the other camera.

### 10.2 What constitutes one dataset sample

One benchmark sample is not an arbitrary pair of camera images. It is a
manifest-confirmed tuple:

```text
(benchmark_frame_id,
 manifest_output_frame,
 camera_1_frame,
 camera_2_frame,
 stitched_panorama,
 person_boxes,
 track_ids,
 review_evidence)
```

For example, benchmark frame 654 in the normal-walking sequence maps to
manifest output frame 654, camera-1 frame 654, and camera-2 frame 654. The two
raw fisheye images are calibrated, dewarped, aligned, and blended into one
panorama image. The benchmark annotations then reference this contiguous
panorama frame ID. `stitching_manifest.csv` preserves the reverse mapping, so a
questionable label can always be traced back to both raw-camera observations.

<p align="center">
  <img src="./report_assets/panorama_source_to_stitch_30102026.jpg"
       alt="Two synchronized raw fisheye frames converted into one stitched panorama sample"
       width="100%" />
  <br />
  <em>Figure 5 — Real dataset example: synchronized camera-1 and camera-2 fisheye frames are transformed into one traceable panorama sample.</em>
</p>

The saved dataset has three layers:

1. **Raw evidence:** every decoded image in `cam1/` and `cam2/`, including
   unmatched frames that cannot be stitched safely.
2. **Synchronized panorama sequence:** `stitching_result/` contains only
   manifest-confirmed pairs, numbered contiguously from 1 for MOT compatibility.
3. **Annotation and review layer:** `img1/`, MOT/YOLO labels, per-frame JSONL,
   track summaries, review events, and rendered evidence for manual correction.

All five capture sessions are marked `invalid` by the strict recorder because
they did not reach the configured 180-second duration and each contains at
least one unmatched camera frame. This status does not mean that every frame is
corrupt. It means the complete recording cannot be claimed to be a perfectly
paired 180-second sequence. All raw frames remain available; only the 6,969
manifest-confirmed pairs are admitted to the panorama benchmark.

The `crossing` sequence is the largest synchronization exception: camera 1 has
86 frames without a camera-2 counterpart. These frames are retained in the raw
camera export but are correctly excluded from stitching.

### 10.4 Offline stitching result

Offline stitching uses the calibrated camera profiles, SeamUNet blending, and
per-session startup alignment. The result for all five sequences was:

| Scenario | Auto vertical | Effective vertical shift | Auto horizontal | Effective right crop | Stitched frames |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Normal walking | Fallback | 20 | Locked | 67 | 1,307 |
| Crossing | Fallback | 20 | Locked | 70 | 1,532 |
| Seam interaction | Fallback | 20 | Locked | 68 | 1,248 |
| Partial occlusion | Fallback | 20 | Locked | 68 | 1,053 |
| Full occlusion | Fallback | 20 | Locked | 68 | 1,829 |

Auto-horizontal alignment locked successfully in every sequence and selected a
stable right crop between 67 and 70 pixels. Auto-vertical alignment did **not**
meet its acceptance criteria in these recordings, so every sequence used the
configured fallback shift of 20 pixels. This is recorded explicitly rather
than presenting the vertical estimate as an automatic-alignment success.

For each sequence, the image export contains:

- `cam1/`: every decoded frame from camera 1;
- `cam2/`: every decoded frame from camera 2;
- `stitching_result/`: one panorama image per synchronized pair;
- `stitching_manifest.csv`: mapping from contiguous benchmark frame ID to the
  original manifest frame and both source-camera frame IDs;
- `export_metadata.json`: frame counts, alignment states, effective offsets,
  model mode, and output validation.

### 11.1 Detector and tracker configuration

The first pseudo-label pass uses:

| Component | Configuration |
|---|---|

| Input size | 1,536 pixels |

## 12. Human-reviewed MOT ground truth and dataset statistics

### 12.1 Manual annotation completion and audit

The five CVAT MOT exports were converted into a sequence-disjoint MOTChallenge
dataset after removing the first 300 and final 100 frames of every sequence.
This avoids the recording-start interaction and reduces redundant annotation
cost. The source exports remain unchanged in `annotation_mot/`; the prepared
derivative is stored in
[`object_tracking_prepared_manual_dataset`](../../cvat_packages_trim300_last100/object_tracking_prepared_manual_dataset/).

The prepared set contains **4,969 panorama frames**, **8,518 manually reviewed
person boxes**, and **10 sequence-local tracks**. Its mechanical audit reports:

- 0 invalid or out-of-image boxes;
- 0 duplicate `(frame, track_id)` rows;
- 0 MOT rows unmatched to the separately exported manual detection boxes;
- all image files and annotation rows resolve inside their declared sequence.

This is a ground-truth tracking set for the trimmed subset, not a relabeling of
all 6,969 bootstrap frames. Track IDs are verified only within a sequence.
`scenario:track_id` is a unique database key and must not be interpreted as a
cross-video ReID identity.

Across all annotations, the median person box occupies only **0.554%** of the
panorama area (IQR **0.146–1.530%**) and has a median height of **18.04%** of the
image. The held-out full-occlusion sequence is especially difficult: its median
box area is **0.161%** and median box height is **8.30%**. In contrast, crossing
has the largest median person area (**1.235%**) and the strongest interactions:
**102 frames** have two-person box IoU ≥0.10 and **10 frames** reach IoU ≥0.30.

Of 4,969 frames, 4,783 (**96.3%**) contain at least one annotated person. The
frame composition is 186 empty frames (3.7%), 1,048 one-person frames (21.1%),
and 3,735 two-person frames (75.2%). The spatial-density plot also shows that
most trajectories occupy the central band of the panorama, so these data do
not yet provide uniform 360-degree spatial coverage.

### 12.4 Identity continuity and qualitative examples

The annotations contain **74 within-track temporal gaps**. Nine gaps last at
least 30 frames, and the longest verified return is **220 frames** in the
full-occlusion test sequence. A missing row means that the identity is not
visibly annotated in that frame; the same sequence-local ID resumes when the
person returns. This structure makes the dataset directly useful for testing
ID-switch recovery and long-term identity association.

### 12.5 What the manual set can and cannot support

The dataset is ready for sequence-level MOT evaluation, box detection
evaluation, ID-switch analysis, and long-gap reassociation experiments. It is
not yet sufficient for supervised visibility estimation: MOT column 9 is 1.0
for every visible box, while complete absence is represented by no row. It is
also not a cross-camera or cross-video ReID classification set because physical
identity equivalence across the five recordings has not been verified.

## 13. Current limitations

1. The manual subset has passed deterministic consistency checks, but a second
   independent annotator/adjudication pass has not yet been recorded. Paper
   claims should distinguish “manually annotated and audited” from
   “multi-annotator consensus.”
2. Visibility severity is not labeled. Partial visibility, full occlusion, and
   out-of-frame absence cannot be separated from MOT column 9 alone.
3. The dataset has only five videos and two sequence-local tracks per video.
   The current sequence-disjoint split prevents adjacent-frame leakage but does
   not establish broad scene, subject, or camera generalization.
4. The original pseudo-label pipeline produces detections and tracks in one
   `model.track()` path. It can omit unassigned detector candidates and remains
   unsuitable as ground truth for the 2,000 frames outside the manual subset.
5. The strict capture validator rejected all five sessions as complete
   recordings. The synchronized subsets are usable, but capture transport still
   needs investigation, especially the 86 unmatched crossing frames.
6. Auto-vertical alignment fell back in every sequence. The fixed shift is
   auditable, but fully automatic vertical alignment has not been demonstrated.
7. HIVE + ECIA has now been validated through oracle-triggered reassociation
   and replay on held-out QuadTrack videos, but not on this panorama ground
   truth. No IDSW, IDF1, HOTA, or MOTA improvement is claimed on the new
   dataset yet.
8. ECIA training overfits after epoch 3–4, and the learned gate only weakly
   separates correct from incorrect HIVE selections. Results currently cover
   one fixed seed, four test videos, and 92 oracle-triggered events.

## 14. Next steps

1. Perform a second QA pass on crossings, seam contacts, track boundaries, and
   all 74 gaps; record reviewer/adjudicator provenance.
2. Add explicit `visibility_state` attributes (`visible`, `partial_occlusion`,
   `full_occlusion`, `out_of_frame`) without overloading the MOT visibility
   scalar.
3. Freeze dataset version and checksums, then run YOLO + ByteTrack and at least
   one stronger detector/tracker baseline on the fixed train/val/test split.
4. Tune all thresholds on the seam validation sequence only; report detection
   precision/recall, MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches, fragmentation, and per-gap
   recovery on the untouched full-occlusion test sequence.
5. Preserve raw detector candidates independently from tracker assignments for
   future annotation and error analysis.
6. Transfer the frozen HIVE + ECIA replay protocol to the manually reviewed
   panorama split; tune only on the seam-validation video and preserve the
   full-occlusion sequence as untouched test data.
7. Repeat ECIA with multiple seeds, stronger regularization, and explicit
   gate-quality analysis; report confidence intervals and per-sequence failure
   cases rather than selecting an ablation from held-out performance.
8. Diagnose capture interruption and improve auto-vertical alignment before
   collecting a larger, more diverse second dataset release.

## Weekly conclusion

This week produced one negative modeling result, one positive identity-recovery
result, and two systems milestones. The
controlled HIVE + HSV ablation showed that the color bridge should not be
retained: it reduces held-out ranking quality and more than doubles selection
latency. Frozen HIVE v2 + Lightweight ECIA subsequently reduced held-out
QuadTrack ID switches from 92 to 45 while raising IDF1 and HOTA; the result is
promising but remains oracle-triggered, shows rapid training overfit, and has
not been tested on the new panorama ground truth. In parallel, the target dual-fisheye workflow produced 6,969 auditable
synchronized panoramas and an evidence-aware pseudo-label bootstrap. The
trimmed subset has now progressed beyond pseudo-labels to a manually annotated
MOT dataset with 4,969 frames, 8,518 boxes, and 10 sequence-local tracks.
Paper-ready scale, geometry, temporal-gap, split, and qualitative figures are
included above. The next research priority is independent annotation QA,
transfer of the fixed ECIA replay protocol to the manual panorama split, and a
non-oracle switch-triggering baseline; no tracking improvement on the new data
is claimed until replay is evaluated against this manual ground truth.
